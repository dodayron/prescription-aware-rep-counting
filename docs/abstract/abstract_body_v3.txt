Adherence to home exercise is largely self-reported, and existing measures rarely capture the prescribed dose. Camera-based repetition counters could supply objective data, but they apply fixed, fitness-derived thresholds, whereas rehabilitation exercise is prescribed to a patient-specific range. We tested whether such counters misreport patients' exercise, and whether reporting attempts (adherence) separately from attempts at prescribed depth (fidelity) corrects this.
A two-state joint-angle rule using MediaPipe BlazePose (lite) on a Raspberry Pi 5 (18–22 fps) was extended with auto-calibration (thresholds from the user's first two repetitions) and a physiotherapist-set depth band (default 60–70° knee flexion). In this preliminary offline evaluation the rule was applied to KIMORE squats (31 patients, 42 healthy adults; clinician scores; Kinect skeletons, simulated side view), REHAB24-6 squats (9 healthy adults, video with motion capture, same lite model) and IntelliRehabDS lateral raises (14 patients, 14 controls; annotated repetitions). Intervals are subject-level bootstrap 95% CIs.
Fixed thresholds logged zero squats for 58% [42, 74] of KIMORE patients; these 18 patients made 50 attempts, 28 at prescribed depth. Overall the fixed counter logged 35 patient repetitions; the calibrated counter recorded 112 attempts, 87 at prescription (fidelity 78% [64, 89]; healthy 99%). Fidelity correlated with clinician score (ρ = 0.44 [0.19, 0.62]). In IntelliRehabDS the fixed rule registered 77% [55, 98] of patient repetitions that annotators judged correct, against 98% [95, 100] with calibration. Against motion capture, calibration raised recall from 54% to 97% [93, 100] with no false positives; knee-angle RMSE fell from 15.8° to 4.5° [4.2, 4.9] with a linear correction learned on other participants.
Fixed-threshold counting can report prescribed exercise as non-adherence. Counting attempts and reporting depth against the prescription gives clinicians adherence and fidelity from one single-board device. Validation on the device with patients, under ethics approval, is the next step.
