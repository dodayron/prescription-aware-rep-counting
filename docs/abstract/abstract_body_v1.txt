Consumer repetition counters apply fixed gym thresholds, but rehabilitation exercise is prescribed to a patient-specific range of motion. We asked whether separating counting (adherence) from quality (depth relative to the prescribed band) makes a camera counter usable for prescribed squats.
The system runs MediaPipe BlazePose (lite) on a Raspberry Pi 5 with a wide-angle camera and counts repetitions from the 2D knee angle with a two-state rule. We added auto-calibration (thresholds set from two repetitions at the prescribed depth, 25% margin) and a physiotherapist-configurable prescription band (default 60–70° knee flexion). We evaluated the rule offline, with the same lite model, on REHAB24-6 (9 healthy adults, 195 annotated squats, RGB video with optical motion capture) for measurement accuracy, and KIMORE (squat exercise; 42 healthy adults, 31 patients with stroke, Parkinson's disease or low back pain; clinician scores) for clinical transfer, using Kinect skeletons projected to a simulated side view. Rates carry subject-level bootstrap 95% CIs.
In profile view, knee-angle bias against motion capture was +14.3° (RMSE 15.7°). Calibrated thresholds counted 97% of annotated squats versus 54% with fixed thresholds (160°/90°), with no false positives. In KIMORE patients, recordings in which fixed thresholds counted zero repetitions fell from 58% [42, 74] to 0% with calibration, and recordings counted within ±1 rose from 26% to 81% [65, 94]. Calibrated depth correlated with clinician score (ρ = −0.31 [−0.54, −0.07], p = 0.008, n = 73). On the Pi 5 the lite model ran at 18–22 fps at 640×480.
Of 18 patients scored as zero by fixed thresholds, 10 had met the prescribed depth; calibration counts all 18 and the band flags the 8 shallow performers. Counting every attempt and reporting depth against the prescription separates adherence from quality. This is a preliminary offline evaluation; a prospective home study with ethics approval is the next step.
